The Place

Turn off the road
and the noise stops

A wooden cabin with the lights on. Thatched huts, tables under the trees, and open field running out to the treeline. There is a horse. There is a buffalo farm along the fence and water beyond it.

In the evening the string lights come on, someone picks up a guitar, and nobody is in a hurry to leave. That is the whole idea.

MEMC Wild Roots Farmhouse opened in 2026 on the edge of Manahara in Ratnanagar, Chitwan — about three kilometres from Sauraha and ten from Bharatpur. It is a farmhouse in the Nepali sense: somewhere to give an afternoon and an evening to, eat well, sit outside, and stay for the fire.

No park permit. No gate fee. No jeep to book.You are three kilometres short of the Sauraha gate and none of that applies here. Turn off the road and you have arrived.

The Grounds

What is actually here

No brochure language. This is what you will find when you walk in.

A horse

Guests tend to meet the horse before they meet the menu. It is the first thing people mention afterwards.

Open field to the treeline

Grass, saplings and space, running out to standing forest. Buffalo farm along the fence, water beyond it.

The machan

A raised thatched platform, the way they are built across the Terai. Best seat on the property when the sun drops.

Fire and string lights

After dark the lights come on across the huts and the lane, and the fire gets going. This is when the place is at its best.

Somebody playing

A guitar under the thatch, unamplified, no stage. It happens because people bring instruments, not because it is scheduled.

Room to park

Off the road, room for cars and a line of bikes. Riding groups can pull in together without blocking anything.

Riders

The motorcycle on our badge
is not decoration

From Road To Roots. That is the whole name of the thing.

Chitwan sits in the middle of every good loop in this country — Kathmandu down through Mugling, across to Pokhara, out to Lumbini and back. MEMC is a few minutes off that line, with somewhere to put the bikes, food that does not take an hour, ground to pitch a tent on and a fire to sit around after.

Club runs, weekend groups, solo riders passing through. Give us a call before you set off and we will have the fire ready.

Owner to supply — the eight answers that close a group booking Every group organiser in Nepal asks the same questions on the first call. Answer these once and they go on the page permanently, and most of them stop needing a call at all:
  1. Maximum headcount you can seat and feed
  2. Minimum group size for a booking
  3. How much notice you need — normal group vs barbecue
  4. Can a group bring its own food or cook on site?
  5. Can they bring their own drinks?
  6. How many toilets, and where
  7. Can a bus get down the access road, and how many vehicles fit
  8. Veg and non-veg both available?
Owner to supply — one price anchor Not a full menu, just a floor: “Meals from around NPR ___. Group day packages from around NPR ___ per head.” A range beats silence — a family deciding whether to drive 30 minutes from Bharatpur needs a number, and a range is not a legally displayed price so it does not trap you. For reference, Bharatpur halls publish NPR 300–900 per plate and the free public picnic grounds charge NPR 20–200 entry.
